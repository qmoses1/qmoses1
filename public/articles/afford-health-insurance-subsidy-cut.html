You don't have to go without coverage: 3 ways to still afford major medical — even if your subsidy got cut

If your premium jumped this year and feels impossible, don't drop your coverage. There are real ways to keep major medical protection on a tighter budget. Here are three.

1. Raise your deductible — then backfill the gap with cheap supplemental coverage

A higher-deductible major-medical plan costs much less per month. The catch is a bigger deductible if something serious happens — so you fill that gap with inexpensive accident, critical-illness, and hospital-indemnity policies that pay you cash if you're hospitalized, injured, or diagnosed with something major. Net result: a lower monthly cost, and you're still protected against the catastrophe.

Important: these supplemental policies are not comprehensive coverage and not a replacement for major medical — they don't count as minimum essential coverage. You keep your major medical plan; the supplements only backfill the deductible gap. Anyone telling you to drop major medical for these is steering you wrong.

2. Make sure you're claiming every subsidy dollar — on the right plan

Premium tax credits still exist in 2026, just smaller. People routinely under-claim by mis-estimating income or picking the wrong plan. If your income is in the right range, a Silver plan with cost-sharing reductions can be cheaper and meaningfully better than the lowest sticker price. In California, state subsidies stack on top of federal ones. Re-running the numbers often drops your real cost.

3. Right-size the plan to your actual life

If you're generally healthy, a Bronze or high-deductible plan paired with an HSA can cut the premium substantially — and the HSA lets you save for medical costs with triple tax advantages (confirm details with your tax advisor). If your income recently dropped, check Medicaid: you may qualify for genuinely free coverage. The cheapest premium isn't always the cheapest year — matching the plan to your real usage is the move.
