Your claim was denied. Here's exactly how to fight it — and who helps for free.

A denial is not the final answer. Roughly 4 in 10 internal appeals succeed, the appeal costs nothing, and if the insurer says no twice, an independent external review is free and legally binding on them.

First: don't pay the bill yet

Denials are often a coding error, a missing prior authorization, or a "not medically necessary" flag — all appealable. Panicking and paying is the one move that can't be undone.

The four steps

  1. Get the denial in writing. Your Explanation of Benefits (EOB) shows the exact reason and code for the denial.
  2. Call the insurer and ask precisely why — and what documentation would overturn it.
  3. File the internal appeal before the deadline (often 180 days). A letter of medical necessity from your doctor is the strongest thing you can attach.
  4. Denied again? Request an external review. It's handled by an independent third party, it's free, and the decision is binding on your insurer.

Who helps — free or low-cost

  • Patient Advocate Foundation — free case management for complex appeals.
  • Counterforce Health — free AI-generated appeal letters built from clinical guidelines.
  • Your state's Consumer Assistance Program or Attorney General health helpline.
  • Cancer Support Community and disease-specific nonprofits — appeal guidance and advocacy.
  • For the bill itself: Dollar For (free help applying for hospital charity care), PAN Foundation and HealthWell Foundation (copay help for serious illness).
