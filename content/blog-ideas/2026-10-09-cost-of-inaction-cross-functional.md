# Blog idea: the cost of inaction rarely stops at one function

Status: HELD (raw notes, not drafted)
Captured: 2026-10-09
Open item before drafting: name and link the survey this comes from.

## Raw notes (verbatim)

The survey's central tension was that the cost of inaction usually reached more than one part of the buyer's company, while sellers tended to isolate the problem to the one function in front of them. The guidance was to understand the full problem and who else stands to benefit, because that builds internal advocacy, which isolating the problem doesn't. Sellers should expect or dig for cross-functional questions and use them to build rapport. The frameworks are sales-agnostic. In B2B insurance sales, a benefits change may be a finance initiative, but could there also be a talent acquisition or retention initiative? What impact will your new traffic source have on brand safety and identity? Teams used the findings to create informative content; sales used it for script structure, account research and sales training templates.

## Angles already in the notes
